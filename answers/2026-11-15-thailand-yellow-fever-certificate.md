**The rule:** Thailand asks for the certificate from travellers who "have travelled from or through" a yellow fever country, and Brazil is on the list. The trigger is the journey, not the passport. You land in Chiang Mai from Chongqing after 27 days in China, so on the literal rule it is not needed. But Brazilian nationals are told to carry proof that they don't live in an infected area, and you live in Brazil. The certificate is free, so carry it (PDF on the phone plus a printout).

**Europe:** no European country asks for it.

**The vaccine in Brazil:** on the SUS calendar at 9 months, with a booster at 4 years. Many adults got it in the 2017–2018 campaigns. One full dose is valid for life on the international certificate. A 2018 *fractional dose* (dose fracionada) is **not** valid for the certificate: take a full dose.

**Getting the shot:**
- Walk in at the sala de vacina of a UBS (posto de saúde). It's free and usually needs no appointment.
- Bring a photo ID, your CPF or SUS card, and the vaccination card if you have one.
- Some postos only open the multi-dose vial on certain days or shifts, so call first or go in the morning.
- Tell them about a severe egg allergy, immunosuppressing medicine, or autoimmune or cancer treatment.
- A day or two of mild fever or aches is common, starting 3 to 7 days after the shot.
- Ask them to register the dose in the system so it reaches Meu SUS Digital.
- The certificate is valid only 10 days after the dose.

**Getting the certificate (CIVP):**
1. Log in to the Meu SUS Digital app or meususdigital.saude.gov.br with gov.br and look for the yellow fever CIVP. Download the PDF.
2. If the dose doesn't show (common for shots before 2023), use the gov.br form (in the sources) and attach a photo or PDF scan of the paper card page. gov.br says the answer takes from minutes up to 5 business days (as of Nov 2026).
