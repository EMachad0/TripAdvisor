**Printed bookings: skip.** No country on the route asks for paper. Save the PDFs for offline use on both phones (main and backup): flights, the ticket out of each country, the first night's stay, insurance. The backup phone does the job the printout did.

**Reusable water bottle: skip.** It is a comfort choice. Tap water is not for drinking in China or Thailand anyway, and bottled water is sold everywhere on the route. If a hostel has a dispenser, refill a bought bottle.

**Cash: carry some, in local money.** Bringing dollars from Brazil to exchange is not needed. Going with none at all does not work:

- **Japan:** the phone is a Galaxy bought outside Japan, so Suica can't live on it until 2027 (JR East). The physical card is topped up with yen cash at stations and convenience stores. Many ramen ticket machines, shrines, small restaurants and coin lockers take only cash. In a 2024 Japan Tourism Agency survey, 94.5% of visitors paid cash at some point. Keep about ¥10,000 (≈ R$ 369) on you.
- **Thailand:** street food, markets and songthaews are cash. Foreign cards pay ฿250 (≈ R$ 43) per ATM withdrawal with Visa and ฿350 (≈ R$ 61) with Mastercard under the 2026 bank fee tables, so withdraw a large amount once rather than small ones often. The fee is shown on the screen before you confirm.
- **Thailand, at the border:** an officer may ask for proof of funds. The official amount is ฿10,000 (≈ R$ 1.730) per person for "certain visa-exempt cases"; some embassies publish ฿20,000 (≈ R$ 3.460). It can be baht, another currency, or documents that show the money. Checks are rare. Have the bank app ready to show a balance.
- **Korea:** T-money top-ups at convenience stores are cash only. Some new Seoul subway kiosks take foreign cards, but sources disagree on whether that covers top-ups, so don't count on it.
- **China:** the one country where no cash works, through Alipay and WeChat Pay with a foreign card. The weak point is the phone: a dead battery or no signal means no money. ¥200 (≈ R$ 176) in a pocket covers a taxi and a meal.

**What to do:** in each country, withdraw at an airport ATM on arrival, with a card that has low foreign fees. Hide one US$100 note apart from the wallet. It weighs nothing and it still works when a card is blocked, an ATM keeps the card or the phone is gone.

Rates used: 1 THB = R$ 0,173, 1 JPY = R$ 0,0369, 1 CNY = R$ 0,8807 (ECB reference rates via frankfurter.dev, 24 Nov 2026).
