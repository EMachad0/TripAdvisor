**Which show.** Shuyanfu, 蜀宴赋, at EKA·Tianwu Park: 1F, Building 38, 535 Jinqiao Road, Pudong. Metro Line 6, Jinqiao Road, Exit 3, then a 10–15 min walk. Sessions at lunch and dinner, daily; search results give 12:30 and 19:00 UTC+8 (not confirmed on the venue's own page).

**Is it open to foreigners?** Yes. Several English-language reviewers bought tickets on Trip.com, and no passport or Chinese phone rule is mentioned anywhere. Every guest has their own small table, and one reviewer booked a single seat, so going solo is normal. Weekends sell out: book about a week ahead.

**Dress-up for men.** This is the least documented part.

- Styling is a paid extra, done in a separate building: about ¥298 (≈ R$ 253) for costume and hair accessories, about ¥398 (≈ R$ 338) with professional makeup. No separate men's price was found for Shanghai; the Chengdu original sells a men's ticket for ¥198.
- The wardrobe is 200–300 Tang, Song and Ming outfits. One review praises "the men's armor", another says men's hanfu is rented, but most reviewers and photos are women. Expect a robe and a hat or hairpiece, with a smaller choice than the women get.
- Arrive 1 to 1.5 hours before the show to change. Dressing up is optional.

**Is the show Chinese only?** Spoken parts are in Chinese. Screens carry English text for the story, which one reviewer found hard to read from the back rows, and staff English is weak. Most of the seven acts are dance, drums, sword and sleeve numbers with a costume parade, so little depends on the words.

**Will I enjoy it without Chinese?** Probably yes. English-language reviewers were positive ("truly loved the evening"), and the food is the strong point: Sichuan dishes toned down in spice, about eight courses. Sit in the front or middle rows to read the screens, and avoid seats beside the pillars. The only complaint that repeats is feeling rushed to finish each dish.

**Cost.** Seats ¥598–698 (≈ R$ 508–593), VIP and SVIP more; a 2025 list had seat plus styling bundles at ¥996–1,096 (≈ R$ 846–930). That is five times the R$ 170 daily China budget, so it is a splurge night; without the costume it is about ¥600 (≈ R$ 509).
