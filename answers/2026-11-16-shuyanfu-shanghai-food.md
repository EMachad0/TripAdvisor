**What is on the plate.** Eight to ten small courses, one per dance act, served as individual portions over about 100 minutes. The kitchen calls it creative cooking built on the 24 flavours of Sichuan cuisine. The menu changes each season, so December will differ, but these dishes recur in Shanghai reviews:

- Peppercorn squab ("Chu-Han")
- Mapo tofu with marbled beef or sea cucumber
- Beef short rib in *guaiwei* ("strange flavour") sauce
- Sea cucumber in golden broth
- A river fish with yellow or pickled chilli
- A duck or chicken soup to start, a fruit pudding to finish

**Is it historical food?** No. Sea cucumber mapo tofu and truffle beef are today's banquet cooking. Chilli itself only reached Sichuan kitchens in the Qing dynasty, so nothing spicy here could be a Han or Tang recipe. No source says the dishes are reconstructed from old texts; one Chengdu reviewer says only that "some" are inspired by ancient recipes. What is historical is the staging: costumes, music, Tang-style gold and silver tableware, and a light show telling a story about each dish.

**How does it taste?** Better than a dinner show usually manages. Shanghai visitors write "I thought entertainment restaurants were all just for show and the food was bad. But I was totally wrong" and "on point without being too oily or salty"; the squab and the fish get the most praise. The heat is turned down for Shanghai and foreign guests, so it is milder than Sichuan food in Sichuan. Two cautions come up: one visitor felt rushed and left food behind, and a Chengdu reviewer's mother, with "picky Chinese tastebuds", found it "just okay".

**How far to trust this.** The praise comes from undated posts on Trip.com, which sells the tickets, plus sponsored articles. I found no independent critic's review, no named chef and no award, and could not read Dianping reviews. Treat "good" as "good for a show".

**Verdict.** The ticket is ¥498 (≈ R$ 423) for a standard lunch seat, at R$ 0.8489 per yuan (ECB rate, 13 Nov 2026), and you are paying mostly for the show. Go if you want the spectacle with a decent meal attached. If the point is tasting, the same money buys far better Sichuan food in Chengdu and Chongqing later on this trip, at full strength.
