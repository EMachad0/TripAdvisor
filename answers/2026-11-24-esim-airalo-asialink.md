**What was bought.** Airalo "Asialink", 100 GB, 180 days, list price US$89 (≈ R$ 522). A 15% first-purchase discount brought it to about US$75.65 (≈ R$ 444). Prices as of Nov 24, 2026; rate 1 USD = R$ 5,87 (ECB reference rate of Nov 24, 2026).

- **Coverage:** one profile for mainland China, Hong Kong, Japan, South Korea, Singapore and Thailand.
- **Top-up:** more data can be added to the same eSIM in the Airalo app. Top-ups cost US$15 for 5 GB or US$25 for 10 GB, 30 days each. Top up before the balance reaches zero, because the app then needs Wi-Fi.
- **Hotspot:** allowed, no cap stated for fixed-data packages.
- **China:** Airalo's help page says packages that include China use international roaming through gateways outside the mainland, with "no VPN required and no filters applied". It does not name the exit country; one third-party report says the Netherlands. Not yet tested on this trip.

**Status.** The profile was installed in Brazil on Nov 24, 2026 and shows in SIM manager. It has no signal in Brazil, which is expected: Asialink has no coverage there, and the 180 days start on first connection in Asia. Never delete the profile; the QR code may not install a second time.

**On landing in Shanghai.**

1. Settings › Connections › SIM manager: switch the Airalo line on and set it as the mobile data SIM. Keep "Data switching" off.
2. Settings › Connections › Mobile networks: turn on data roaming for the Airalo line.
3. Open Google, then an IP-check site to see where the traffic exits.
4. If it does not connect, switch on NuRoaming the same way.

**Phone layout.** The Fold8 Ultra keeps two lines active at once and stores many eSIM profiles (Samsung's general figure is up to 20; not confirmed for this model). The Vivo chip must stay active for SMS, so only one eSIM can be on beside it: Airalo during the trip, with NuCel and NuRoaming switched off but kept.

**NuRoaming as backup.** Nubank's 10 GB covers all six countries, China included. Nubank does not publish the network, the exit point, the hotspot rule or the price of extra packs (the page only says up to 20 GB more), so it is the spare, not the main line.

**VPN.** On this eSIM's mobile data, and on a laptop using the phone's hotspot, no VPN should be needed in China. Hostel and hotel Wi-Fi in China still needs one, on phone and laptop.

**How much data.** Sized at about 70 GB for 66 days: the phone at 1 GB a day in China and 0.5 GB elsewhere (about 47 GB), plus hotspot on roughly a third of the 15 work nights at 0.5 GB an hour (about 25 GB). A full work night on the hotspot is about 4.5 GB.

**What it beat** (totals for the whole trip, as of Nov 24, 2026):

- **Airalo Asialink 50 GB / 90 days:** US$69 (≈ R$ 405). Enough for the phone alone, tight with tethering.
- **Trip.com Asia & Oceania, 50 GB / 30 days:** US$22.94 each, three needed, US$68.82 (≈ R$ 404) for 150 GB. Its page lists the exit IP as Hong Kong or Singapore and hotspot as supported. Plans last 30 days at most, and a renewal starts when the data is used up; the page does not say what happens when the days run out first. The cheapest fallback if Airalo fails.
- **Ubigi Best Asia, 20 GB a month:** US$19 a month, US$57 (≈ R$ 335) for three months. A subscription that can only be cancelled after three months, and Ubigi makes no written claim about the firewall.
- **Nomad APAC:** about US$94 (≈ R$ 549) for 60 GB. Plans last 45 days at most, and new plans cannot be bought inside mainland China, only add-ons.
- **Roamless:** about US$122 (≈ R$ 714) for 100 GB, bought as one plan per country. Wallet credit never expires.
- **Holafly:** US$76.50 per 30 days, US$229.50 (≈ R$ 1.347) for the trip, with hotspot capped at 1 GB a day.
- **CUniq "Chinese Mainland and Macau", 50 GB / 90 days:** HK$226 (≈ R$ 169, at 1 HKD = R$ 0,7485). Cheapest for China, but it cannot be topped up, cannot be activated inside the mainland and covers China only.
- **Saily and Jetpac:** ruled out. Saily's China page says WhatsApp needs a VPN; Jetpac's own blog says Google stays blocked on its China eSIM.

Since mid-2026 Hong Kong carriers (China Mobile HK from Jun 17, CUniq from Aug 1) block activating their prepaid China cards inside the mainland, so a Hong Kong SIM is not something to pick up on arrival.
