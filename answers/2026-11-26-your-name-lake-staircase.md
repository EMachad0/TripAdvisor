Itomori, the comet town, is fictional. The lake view fans match to it is Lake Suwa seen from Tateishi Park, on the hillside above Suwa City in Nagano. The park's grassy terrace steps down toward the lake like an open-air theatre, and that is the staircase-with-the-lake photo.

**Address:** 〒392-0003 長野県諏訪市上諏訪10399 立石公園. It is about 1.5 km uphill from JR Kami-Suwa Station, roughly 30 minutes on foot. The old local bus is now an on-demand service that needs registration (¥500, ≈ R$ 19), so plan to walk or take a taxi (about 15 minutes; price not published).

**From Tokyo, as a day trip:**

- Alpico highway bus from Busta Shinjuku to Kami-Suwa Station: ¥3,500 (≈ R$ 130) each way online, ¥3,800 (≈ R$ 141) at the counter. Round trip ¥7,000 (≈ R$ 259).
- The 07:25 UTC+9 bus arrives at 10:27 UTC+9. The last bus back leaves Kami-Suwa at 19:26 UTC+9 and reaches Shinjuku at 22:35 UTC+9, which leaves time for dusk at the park.
- The timetable is the 2025-07-01 revision. Check it again before you go.
- The JR Azusa limited express takes about 2.5 hours from Shinjuku. Its fare could not be confirmed on a JR page.

**At the park:** free, no gate, no listed hours. Toilets are across the road. Dusk into the night view is the best time: in late January the sun sets between 17:02 and 17:17 UTC+9.

**Winter:** Suwa averages −5.5 °C at night with about 27 cm of snow in January, so the lanes up and the terrace steps can be icy. The lake rarely freezes fully now (the last full ice ridge was in 2018), so expect open water or patchy ice.

**Two things that get mixed up:**

- Director Makoto Shinkai said his own image for Itomori Lake came from two small lakes in Koumi, Nagano (Matsubara and Ōtsuki). Lake Suwa is the one fans and Nagano's tourism site compare to the film.
- The stone stairs up to Mitsuha's shrine are Ketawakamiya Shrine in Hida-Furukawa, Gifu, with no lake in sight. The red-railed stairs from the final scene are Suga Shrine in Tokyo.
